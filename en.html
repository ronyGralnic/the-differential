ENGLISH SUMMARY

One problem. Competing solutions. One test.

The Differential is a method made visible. Each case takes a real news story, finds the problem behind it, and lets competing solution ideas fight it out in a step-by-step elimination. Most fall, each for a concrete engineering reason. The survivor gets full documentation: specification, architecture and design. The interactive site is in Hebrew; the documents below are available in English.

CASE 01

Who holds the switch? AI regulation in the US

Eight in ten Americans want AI regulation, and nobody agrees on which. The question that stays open: if a model goes out of control, who holds the switch, and who is allowed to press it? Six solution ideas were eliminated one by one, each for a concrete engineering reason. The winner, Model Kill-Switch Protocol, uses an M-of-N committee signature, an enforcing gateway beside the model and a public append-only log.

CASE 02

A slow-motion crisis: the end of cheap money

Rich-world governments borrowed almost for free for a decade. Now cheap debt is rolling into expensive debt, and the AI giants are borrowing in the same queue. The damage arrives slowly, then all at once, as in the UK in 2022 and at SVB in 2023. Seven solution ideas set out to see the fast part of the crisis before it arrives. The winner, Private Exposure Registry, lets supervised funds and banks split their exposures into three shares so that three regulators can add them up without anyone seeing a single institution's data, and checks every total against cryptographic commitments.

CASE 03

The quiet deletion: 130 million names

A revision of India's voter rolls removed more than 130 million names from the drafts, run centrally with software that showed nobody who was removed and why, and millions had no real chance to appeal in time. Seven solution ideas set out; most fell for concrete reasons. The survivor is a transparency log for the roll: every change recorded with a reason, impossible to alter quietly, cosigned by independent witnesses, and the person whose name changed hears about it the same day.
